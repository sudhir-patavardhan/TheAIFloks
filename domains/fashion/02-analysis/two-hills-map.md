## The commoditizing baseline

Everything in the PA row is purchasable by any competitor on identical terms — that's what makes it baseline rather than moat. The cleanest single illustration: the same trend-forecasting vendor claims both Louis Vuitton and Dior as clients (#14) — one signal, sold to rivals, differentiates neither, and may push output toward convergence (#38). Industry scoreboard confirms: spend rising (tech 1.6–1.8% → 3–3.5% of sales by 2030, #48; AI in luxury top-3 priorities 5% → 22%, #50) while ~60% report no significant measurable AI impact (#50; #51 pending direct verification). The much-cited Zara exception (85% full-price sell-through) is folklore — no primary source found (#10).

## The invention opportunity

- **Previously-impossible problem:** authentication at scale. Counterfeits ≈ $464B / 2.5% of global trade (#36).
- **Constraint AI removes:** authenticity was only inferable *after the fact* by scarce expert inspection — no ground truth existed. Provenance assigned at manufacture (Aura: 40M+ items across LVMH/Prada/Cartier/OTB, #22) turns inference into lookup. Post-hoc CV (Entrupy, #21) automates the old inspection model and leaves the constraint intact — the automation-vs-invention contrast in miniature.
- **Where the intelligence should live:** upstream — in the shared registry the industry writes to at origin, not in a better appraisal model at the end of the chain.
- **Why it compounds / is hard to copy:** the moat is not the blockchain (cheap, old tech) — it's four *competing* conglomerates cooperating on one infrastructure, a coordination cost no startup or single house can casually replicate; every registered item raises the network's value as the default answer to "what is this garment?" The EU's Digital Product Passport mandate (~2028, #75) converts voluntary participation into compulsion.
- **Grilled thesis (surviving form):** durable AI value accrues to systems that compound on an exclusive, hard-to-replicate data network — provenance embedded at manufacture is fashion's clearest instance. Scope honesty: luxury-led today (per-item identity amortizes on a $5,000 bag, not a $12 tee); regulation, not economics, extends it down-market. The resale-side query layer is unbuilt — reframed as an **open opportunity** and promoted to `shared/unsolved-problems.md`.

## Falsification check

- **Evidence sought that automation IS the moat here:** actively searched (grill attacks 3, 5, 7). The payment-fraud "counterexample" resolved *for* the thesis — Visa/Mastercard's models are durable because of their exclusive transaction network, not the automation. The execution-as-moat rebuttal (Inditex wins with bought tools) is conceded narrowly: operational excellence is real and durable, but it's a management capability, not an AI moat, and its best public evidence is unverified folklore (#10).
- **Result:** no confirmed case found of purchased post-hoc automation functioning as a durable moat in fashion. Three observable falsifiers on the watch list: (a) EU delegated act accepts self-declared databases as compliant passports; (b) an Aura founding member defects; (c) a post-hoc authenticator sustains years of pricing power without network exclusivity.

## Thesis (one paragraph, grilled and surviving)

Fashion's AI spending is concentrated — 10 of 12 mapped initiative categories — in process automation that any competitor can buy on identical terms, which is why spend keeps rising while ~60% of companies report no measurable impact. Durable value in this domain accrues instead to systems that compound on an exclusive, hard-to-replicate data network, and the clearest instance is provenance embedded at the point of manufacture: the Aura consortium's shared registry, soon made compulsory in effect by the EU's Digital Product Passport mandate, turns authentication from expert inference into ground-truth lookup — luxury-led today, regulation-extended tomorrow, with the resale-side verification layer standing open as the industry's most concrete unbuilt opportunity.

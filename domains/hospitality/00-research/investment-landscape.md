# Current AI/Tech Investment Landscape — Hospitality

Goal: map where money and attention are going in this domain, and classify it into the 2x2. "Hospitality" here is read primarily through lodging — the global hotel groups (Marriott, Hilton, IHG, Hyatt, Accor, Wyndham, Choice), the OTA/distribution layer (Booking Holdings, Expedia, Airbnb, Trip.com) and the new AI surfaces sitting above it (Google AI Mode, ChatGPT/Claude apps), plus the hotel-tech stack the properties actually run on (Oracle OPERA, Mews, Cloudbeds; IDeaS, Duetto; Canary; Actabl) — and secondarily through restaurants/food service where AI spend is material (McDonald's, Yum, Wendy's, Starbucks, Chipotle, Domino's, DoorDash, Uber Eats). All source numbers refer to `sources.md` (#1–#62 for this pass).

## Funding & spend snapshot

- **Total AI investment in domain:** No credible "AI in hospitality" market-size figure survived verification; what exists is (a) venture funding into hospitality-*tech* startups and (b) the hotel groups' own platform capex. Abode Worldwide's Hospitality Tech Investment Index counts ~$1B across 40 startups for Apr 2025–Mar 2026, of which property-management systems took $408M (7 companies) and "AI-led guest experience platforms" $153M (4 companies) (#38). The single largest event is Mews's $300M Series D at a $2.5B valuation (Jan 2026), explicitly for "agent-driven systems across the platform" (#37); Canary's $80M Series D (Jun 2025, ~$600M valuation) is the guest-messaging/voice analogue (#39).
- **Hotel-group capex on the AI substrate:** Marriott guided ~$1.1B of 2026 investment spending with more than a third (other coverage: "up to 40%") for digital transformation — replatforming its PMS, central reservations and loyalty systems, with 2,000+ select-service hotels migrated by mid-2026 and the cost "reimbursed over time" by owners (#2, #3, #20). Wyndham cites $450M+ in technology since 2018 as the enabler of its AI rollout (#15, #16). IHG is finishing an eight-year systems rebuild — cloud PMS in 4,000 hotels by end-2026, an AI revenue-management system across its entire estate (#10). Hilton attributes its speed to a pre-COVID cloud/microservices stack rather than to new AI spend (#8). Trip.com and Airbnb both flagged AI expense ramping through 2026 without giving numbers (#26, #29).
- **Enterprise budget trend:** A vendor-commissioned survey of 400+ hospitality IT decision-makers has 85% planning at least 5% of IT budget for AI and 58% at least 10%, but only 51% actually piloting or adopted (#40). Cross-industry, Deloitte finds 38% piloting agentic AI and 11% in production (#45); a hospitality-specific study puts "true AI agents" at 11% of hotel organizations (#36). Phocuswright has 61% of travel businesses experimenting with or scaling agentic AI, only 6% "actively scaling" (#34); McKinsey/Skift's earlier read was ~90% of the industry experimenting with generative AI while "few organizations have seen the value reflected in their P&Ls" (#46). The sell side (J.P. Morgan, Mar 2026) calls 2026 the first year hotel AI turns into earnings — while flagging "risks to hotel brand differentiation as AI becomes more widespread" (#20).
- **QSR:** Yum's Byte platform drove ~$9B of digital sales in Q2 2026 (61% mix) and voice AI to 900+ Taco Bell restaurants (#50); McDonald's is rolling Google Edge Cloud hardware to U.S. restaurants ahead of an ArchIQ deployment currently at five test stores (#48). Restaurant robotics capital is flowing to shared vendors (Chipotle $15M and Cava $10M into Hyphen, #57), and in one case out — Sweetgreen sold its Infinite Kitchen unit to Wonder for $186M (#58).

## Claims that don't fit the 2x2 — structural context instead

- **Who owns the transaction.** OpenAI pulled native Instant Checkout in March 2026 (#33) and Google's AI Mode hotel booking launched in September 2026 with the chain or OTA as merchant of record and no autonomous booking (#30, #31). The coordination layer is being built as an open protocol (UCP for Lodging, interoperable with A2A/MCP) with the same names on both sides — Amadeus, Booking, Expedia, Hilton, Marriott, Trip.com co-developing; Accor, Choice, IHG, Wyndham endorsing (#32). This is the ad-servers pattern (Prebid, AdCP/ARTF) arriving pre-emptively: nobody expects to own the rail.
- **Who is excluded.** Independents are absent from Google's launch partner list (#30) while already sending 63.4% of their bookings through OTAs at double the cancellation rate of direct and seeing RevPAR fall 5.4% in 2025 (#35). The AI surfaces are, so far, reinforcing the chain/OTA duopoly over distribution rather than disintermediating it.
- **Loss of interface control.** Expedia's Gorin: on some LLM surfaces "we can control a bit more what the interface is, where our brand shows up, and others where we can't" (#25). Search engines fell from 51% to 36% as the most-used travel-research resource in ~18 months (#34).
- **The cycle, not AI.** 2026 U.S. RevPAR is forecast +4.4% on pricing power and World Cup demand, slowing to +2.1% in 2027 (#47). Sell-side "AI payoff" claims (#20) are not separable from that.
- **Demand-side resistance in QSR.** 80% of consumers prefer a human order-taker; businesses think AI preference is ~40% when it is 22% (#53). McDonald's already ended one 100-store voice-AI pilot over order errors (#48).

## How AI is actually being used — by layer

### 1. Hotel-group platforms and loyalty
Every major chain now runs the same three-part playbook: a conversational search tool on its own surfaces (Marriott Ask Bonvoy, Hilton AI Planner, IHG, Wyndham, Choice — #1, #5, #6, #10, #16), a presence inside third-party LLMs (Accor first in Jan 2026, then Wyndham, IHG, Hilton on ChatGPT/Claude/Gemini — #7, #15, #18), and an internal AI program organized by persona (Marriott: guest/associate/owner, #4; IHG: guest acquisition/hotel performance/corporate efficiency, #10; Hilton: efficiency/distribution/guest, #7). Spend is real — Marriott's ~$1.1B with a third-plus on digital (#3), Wyndham's $450M+ (#15) — but it is overwhelmingly *replatforming* (PMS, CRS, loyalty) that the AI then sits on. Disclosed outcomes are thin: Wyndham's Connect+ numbers (25% handle-time improvement, ~300bps incremental direct contribution across 1,100 hotels — #15) are the most quantified; Hilton, two quarters after launching its AI Planner, has disclosed no usage or conversion data (#9). Marriott's CIO describes the target as a "model-agnostic AI chassis" (#4) — the chains are deliberately not betting on a proprietary model. The strongest evidence of an *institutional* asset is the loyalty graph and the direct share it produces: Hilton at 80%+ direct with "control [of] rate inventory availability" (#7), IHG at 67% loyalty penetration of room nights (#10), Marriott at 295M members (#1). Quadrant: tools PA; loyalty graphs IC; chassis/agentic platforms IC candidates pending evidence they outlive their builders.

### 2. OTA/distribution and agentic booking
The OTAs report the domain's most consistent AI outcome — customer-service cost per booking falling at a double-digit rate at Booking, Agoda, Expedia and Airbnb alike (#21, #23, #24, #27), with Airbnb at ~45% of inquiries resolved without a human (#27). Because all four report it, it is baseline. Trip planners (Penny, Booking.com discovery, TripGenie, Layla) carry no disclosed conversion lift (#22, #23, #25, #29); Expedia's candid framing is that natural-language experiences yield "60% more information regarding traveler intent" but "do not currently drive conversion" (#25). Where the OTAs *do* claim exclusivity it is data: Booking's connected-trip transactions growing at twice the base with higher return frequency and Genius L2/L3 at a high-50% share of room nights (#21); Airbnb's identity-verified two-sided graph (#26). Above the OTAs, Google AI Mode hotel booking went live Sep 2026 with 10 partners and the partner as merchant of record (#30) on an open protocol (#31) after OpenAI abandoned native checkout (#33); Trip.com is explicitly exposing inventory and transaction capability to third-party agents via MCP (#29). Quadrant: CS agents and planners PA; connected trip and verified graph IC; agentic surfaces structural.

### 3. Revenue management and pricing
Vendor RMS is the textbook shared engine: IDeaS serves 16,000 clients including Marriott, IHG and Accor (#42); Duetto and RateGain now push price decisions to channels autonomously (#43); Cloudbeds markets a "Signals" model to independents (#35). The one candidate for something more is IHG's AI-enhanced RMS rolled out across its whole estate over 18 months with "revenue uplift and market share gains" (#10) — franchisor-run pricing on the franchisor's own demand data, which competitors' owners cannot buy. That claim is unquantified. Choice's RAISE and Wyndham's owner tools (#16) are the same idea as franchisor toolkits. Quadrant: PA, with IHG as the IC candidate to test.

### 4. Guest-facing service, messaging and chatbots
Canary sells AI voice, webchat and messaging to 20,000+ hotels including Marriott and Wyndham (#39); Wyndham brands the same capability as its "proprietary" Connect+ (#15); Accor's AI Travel Concierge resolves 40% of queries (#19); Booking is shipping AI messaging to accommodation partners (#22); Oracle bundles an assistant into OPERA at no extra cost (#41). A vendor survey has guest communications as the top expected-impact area (#40). Quadrant: PA throughout — the capability is available identically to every property.

### 5. Operations, housekeeping and labor
Evidence is sparse and vendor-reported: Actabl's predictive labor module cut overtime share 13% across 100+ beta hotels (#44); Accor's PMS assistant pilot *expects* 25% fewer support requests and 30–60 minutes saved per employee per day (#19); Mews raised $300M to build agents that "reduce cognitive load for staff" (#37). The labor effect is visible at the corporate layer: Hyatt cut 30% of U.S. customer-service staff (#14), and its CEO speaks of finding "tremendous levels of efficiency in how we're staffed" while measuring AI success by employee "absorption" (#12, #14). Quadrant: PA.

### 6. F&B / QSR
Drive-thru voice AI is a two-vendor market: Google (McDonald's ArchIQ at five test stores after the failed IBM pilot; Wendy's FreshAI at 160+ stores targeting 500+ — #48, #52) and NVIDIA (Taco Bell at 900+ stores — #49, #50). McDonald's is pre-installing Google Edge hardware nationwide before it has confirmed a single outcome (#48); consumer preference runs 80/20 for humans (#53). The more interesting claim is Yum's: Byte, its proprietary integrated platform, is why voice AI reached 900 restaurants in months and why 61% of sales are digital and first-party (#50, #51) — the platform and data, not the AI, are where Yum locates its edge. Kitchen automation is being funded through shared vendors (Hyphen, #57) or divested outright (Sweetgreen → Wonder, #58). Chipotle's hiring bot (12→4 days, #55, #56) is a purchased product; Starbucks' Green Dot Assist is a barista copilot on Azure OpenAI (#54); Domino's DomOS orchestrates just-in-time production on 2.5B orders of its own timing data (#59). Delivery platforms shipped near-identical cart assistants four months apart (#60, #61). Quadrant: voice AI, robotics, hiring and copilots PA/PP; Byte and Domino's order corpus IC candidates.

### 7. Internal engineering and staff productivity
Airbnb: ~60% of code AI-written, "twice the industry average" (#26, #28); Booking and Expedia cite faster shipping from AI in their tech orgs (#22, #25); Yum has 400+ internal agents and every corporate employee on AI tools (#50); AccorGPT saves 2.5 hrs/week for 7,000 users (#19). Quadrant: PP — real, universal, and non-differentiating.

## Observed concentration

**Confirms the framework's bottom-half expectation, strongly.** Of the ~21 initiative categories above, 13 are PA, 3 are PP, 2 are structural, and only 3 are IC — and those three (loyalty graphs, connected-trip/verified graphs, first-party QSR platforms) are *data assets that predate the generative-AI wave* on which AI features are now being layered. Nothing found in this pass is an AI-native institutional capability with a disclosed, compounding outcome. The domain's signature evidence of commoditization is uniformity: five chains launched equivalent conversational search in one half-year (#5); four OTAs report the same double-digit CS-cost decline (#21–#27); three QSR chains buy drive-thru voice from two vendors (#48–#52); two delivery apps shipped the same cart assistant (#60, #61); two fast-casual rivals fund the same makeline robot (#57). The sell side has noticed — J.P. Morgan's AI-payoff note carries a "brand differentiation" risk flag (#20).

**Where the money is versus where the differentiation is.** The largest disclosed dollars are going to *substrate* — replatformed PMS/CRS/loyalty (Marriott, IHG, Wyndham) and PMS vendors (Mews) — not to models. The chains say so explicitly: "model-agnostic" (#4), "AI-ready technology stack" (#9), "common technology foundation across every channel" (#50). That is consistent with the ad-servers finding that the moat sits one layer up (proprietary intelligence) and one layer over (exclusive data) from the plumbing — except that in hospitality the exclusive data already existed as loyalty and transaction graphs, and the "proprietary intelligence" layer is, so far, purchased.

**Individual Invention is faint.** Hyatt's internally built agentic platforms and RFP tooling (#11, #12) are the only candidates, and Hyatt's own framing — success measured by "absorption" — reads as an attempt to institutionalize them. Marriott's chassis is the same move at larger scale (#4). Whether either becomes IC is the domain's open question.

**Personal Productivity is universal and identical.** AI-written code at Airbnb, Booking and Expedia; staff copilots at Starbucks, Accor and Yum (#19, #26, #28, #50, #54). No one claims these as differentiators.

**The falsification candidate.** The one place automation might *be* the moat is scale of deployment inside a franchise system: Wyndham's 1,100 hotels on Connect+ (#15), IHG's 6,000-hotel RMS (#10), Yum's 900-store voice rollout (#50). If the value is in being able to push a purchased capability across an estate faster than rivals — and owners pay for it through fee reimbursement (#2) — then the moat is the franchise network's deployment rail, not the AI. That is still structural rather than an AI capability, but it deserves the falsification check in `02-analysis`.
